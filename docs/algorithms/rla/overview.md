# RLA 3.0: Robust Learning Algorithms Overview

**RLA (Robust Learning Algorithms)** insulates streaming classifiers from corrupted feedback, mislabeled instances, and asymmetric noise transitions.

---

## 1. Key Capabilities

- **Multi-Signal Reliability Engine (`MultiSignalReliabilityEngine`)**: Combines prediction margins, spatial $k$-NN consensus, and temporal consistency to estimate true sample veracity $r_i \in [0, 1]$.
- **Asymmetric Noise Defense (`AsymmetricTransitionDetector`)**: Estimates empirical transition matrices $T_{kj} = P(\tilde{y}=j \mid y=k)$ to protect vulnerable minority classes from biased annotation corruption.
- **Selective Abstention (`RLA30RobustLearner`)**: Drops or downweights low-reliability samples ($r_i < \tau_{\text{rel}}$) during training while maintaining full operational inference capability.
- **Robust Loss Functions**: PyTorch and NumPy implementations of Generalized Cross-Entropy (`GCE`) and Focal Loss (`FocalLoss`).

---

## 2. When to Use RLA

- Ground-truth labels are generated by imperfect annotators, noisy heuristic rules, or degraded sensors.
- Label corruption is class-asymmetric (e.g. fraud mislabeled as non-fraud).
- Malicious adversaries attempt data poisoning by flipping target labels.
- The pipeline requires verifiable metrics tracking sample retention vs. abstention.
