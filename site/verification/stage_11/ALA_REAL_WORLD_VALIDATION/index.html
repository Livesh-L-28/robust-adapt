    Workstream C: Prequential Streaming Benchmark, Concept Drift Dynamics, and Catastrophic Forgetting Analysis on Electricity (Elec2)¶

    1. Executive Summary & Research Hypothesis¶

    The core hypothesis of the Adaptive Learning Algorithms (ALA) pillar is that dynamically detecting concept drift and adjusting streaming model boundaries produces superior predictive accuracy and recovery times compared to static or naive incremental learners.

    In Stage 11, ALA was benchmarked on the canonical real-world Electricity (Elec2) stream (45,312 sequential instances recorded at 30-minute intervals over 2.5 years). Using a rigorous prequential (test-then-train) protocol across 10 distinct random seeds, AdaptiveML's ALA pipeline was evaluated against three established baselines.

    2. In-Depth Scientific Analysis¶

    2.1 The Catastrophic Forgetting Hazard in Naive Drift Adaptation¶

    A critical discovery emerging from this real-world benchmark is the performance penalty of naive drift resets. - In standard concept drift literature, a common strategy upon ADWIN drift detection is to purge the classifier and instantiate a fresh learner. - On the Electricity dataset, Standard ADWIN Hoeffding Tree triggered an average of \(275\) full resets. - Consequently, its predictive accuracy collapsed from \(74.76\%\) to \(68.66\%\) (a \(-6.10\%\) net penalty). Every time the model encountered non-stationary variance, resetting the tree erased accumulated split statistics, forcing the model to make unconfident predictions during subsequent warm-up phases.

    2.2 Replay Rehearsal Mitigation in AdaptiveML¶

    AdaptiveML's ALA architecture couples ReliabilityWeightedADWIN with a ClassBalancedReservoirReplay buffer: - Upon drift detection, rather than training from a blank slate, the tree is re-primed on a balanced rehearsal sample drawn from historical reservoir memory. - Empirical Impact: AdaptiveML ALA achieved \(72.93\%\) accuracy and \(0.7122\) Macro F1—an absolute improvement of \(+4.27\%\) accuracy (\(p = 1.95 \times 10^{-7}\), Wilcoxon signed-rank test) and \(+0.0371\) Macro F1 over Standard ADWIN. - Furthermore, the reliability-weighting mechanism reduced spurious false alarm resets from \(275.4\) down to \(253.9\) (an \(7.8\%\) reduction in false drift triggers).

    2.3 Transparent Negative Result: Static Tree vs Adaptive Reset¶

    In strict adherence to Stage 11 research principles (Section 8: "Report cases where adaptation makes performance worse"): - The Static Hoeffding Tree (continuous leaf splitting without ever resetting the root) outperformed both adaptive models (\(74.76\%\) vs \(72.93\%\)). - Root Cause Analysis: Electricity pricing dynamics are characterized by recurring seasonal and diurnal concepts (peak vs off-peak hours, weekday vs weekend demand, seasonal pricing). Complete tree resetting discards historical leaves that remain valid for recurring states. - Architectural Recommendation: In non-stationary domains with recurring concepts, full tree purging should be avoided in favor of localized leaf pruning or recurring concept restoration (ConceptMemory).
