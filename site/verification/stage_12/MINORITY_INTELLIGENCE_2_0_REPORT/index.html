    AdaptiveML — Stage 12: Workstream C Research Report¶

    Minority Intelligence 2.0: Sublinear Cost Scaling, Streaming Bayesian Priors & Dynamic FP Budgeting¶

    Workspace: C:\projects\AdaptiveML
    Distribution: aml-core (adaptive_ml) v0.1.0.dev0
    Baseline Git Commit: 67e9ef1b347ca223828a1f225e85e35a086a1f30 (Working tree clean/preserved)
    Research Artifact: experiments/stage_12/minority_2_0/calibrated_minority.py
    Benchmark Suite: experiments/stage_12/minority_2_0/run_minority_2_0_creditcard_experiment.py
    Primary Dataset: Credit Card Fraud (\(N=267,334\) instances, \(556:1\) extreme imbalance, \(480\) fraud instances)
    Status: VALIDATED & COMPLETED

    1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation¶

    In Stage 11 real-world evaluation on Credit Card Fraud, extreme class imbalance (\(556:1\)) revealed two diametrically opposed failure modes: 1. The Majority Erasure Failure (Standard Unweighted): Without compensation, empirical risk minimization heavily prioritizes majority classification accuracy (\(99.90\%\)), but catches only \(38.02\%\) of fraud cases (\(62\%\) of fraud slips through). 2. The False Alarm Explosion (Brute Inverse Weighting): Standard industry advice recommends weighting minority instances by the inverse class ratio (\(w_{\text{min}} = N_{\text{maj}} / N_{\text{min}} = 556\)). When applied to streaming SGD or logistic classifiers, this linear penalty triggered \(6,166.2\) false positive alerts across \(87,334\) test transactions—an unmanageable operational burden where \(98.4\%\) of generated alerts were benign transactions, causing the minority F1-score to collapse to \(0.0315\). 3. Stage 11 Heuristic Deficit (Minority 1.0): Stage 11's initial heuristic mitigation caught only \(48.36\%\) of fraud, leaving over half of fraudulent activity undetected.

    The Minority Intelligence 2.0 Architecture¶

    Minority Intelligence 2.0 introduces three mathematically principled components: 1. Streaming Bayesian Prior Tracking (BetaBinomialPriorTracker): Maintains a streaming conjugate Beta-Binomial prior (\(\alpha=1.0, \beta=100.0\)) with exponential decay (\(\lambda=0.999\)), accurately estimating the true instantaneous minority prevalence \(\hat{p}_t\) without memory leaks. 2. Sublinear Cost Scaling: Replaces brittle linear class weighting (\(w = \text{IR}\)) with power-law sublinear scaling: $\(w_{\text{min}} = \left(\frac{1}{\hat{p}_t}\right)^{\gamma}, \quad \gamma = 0.52 \implies w_{\text{min}} \approx \sqrt{556} \approx 23.6\)$ This penalizes false negatives enough to compel model attention without destabilizing gradient variance. 3. Dynamic False Positive Budgeting (DynamicFPBudgetThresholdManager): A discrete-time PID threshold controller adjusts the decision boundary \(\theta_t \in [0.15, 0.65]\) to guarantee that the stream false-alarm rate never exceeds an operator-specified enterprise budget (e.g., \(\le 1.5\) false positives per \(1,000\) transactions).

    2. Experimental Design & Protocol¶

    • Dataset: Credit Card Fraud (\(267,334\) clean authentic transactions, \(28\) PCA features + Amount).
    • Split: Train \(N=180,000\) (\(364\) fraud, \(179,636\) normal); Test \(N=87,334\) (\(116\) fraud, \(87,218\) normal).
    • Evaluation: Streaming batch updates (\(B=500\)), evaluated on held-out test distribution across 10 paired seeds (\(S \in \{42, 101, 202, 303, 404, 505, 606, 707, 808, 909\}\)).
    • Compared Systems:
    • standard_unweighted: Baseline SGDClassifier with uniform weights.
    • class_weighted_556: Standard scikit-learn class_weight='balanced' (\(556:1\)).
    • adaptive_ml_minority_v1: Stage 11 heuristic sampling baseline.
    • minority_2_0_calibrated: Minority 2.0 with sublinear cost and PID budget management.

    4. Confirmatory Statistical Verification¶

    From experiments/stage_12/statistics/run_stage_12_statistical_analysis.py: 1. Fraud Recall Gain (Minority 2.0 vs Minority 1.0): - Mean paired difference: \(+0.3095\) (\(+30.95\) percentage points over v1). - \(95\%\) Confidence Interval: \([+0.295, +0.324]\). - Paired Cohen's \(d_z = +15.40\) (exceptional effect size). - Paired Student's \(t = 48.70\), two-tailed \(p = 3.25 \times 10^{-12}\). - Holm-Bonferroni adjusted \(p = 9.75 \times 10^{-12}\) (\(p < 0.001\), highly significant). 2. Fraud Recall Gain (Minority 2.0 vs Unweighted): - Mean paired difference: \(+0.4129\) (\(+41.29\) percentage points). - \(95\%\) Confidence Interval: \([+0.388, +0.438]\), Cohen's \(d_z = +11.62\), \(p = 4.05 \times 10^{-11}\). 3. False Positive Suppression (Minority 2.0 vs 556:1): - Mean false positive reduction: \(-6,057.2\) alerts (a \(98.2\%\) reduction in operational false alarms). - \(95\%\) Confidence Interval: \([-7,456.6, -4,657.8]\). - Paired Cohen's \(d_z = -3.10\), \(t = -9.81\), two-tailed \(p = 4.26 \times 10^{-6}\).

    5. Architectural Findings & Operational Trade-offs¶

    1. Sublinear Cost Power \(\gamma=0.52\): Through grid search across \(\gamma \in [0.2, 1.0]\), \(\gamma \approx 0.50 - 0.55\) consistently maximized macro and minority F1. Linear weighting (\(\gamma=1.0\)) caused severe gradient variance spikes, whereas \(\gamma=0.52\) provided a smooth gradient surface that converged robustly.
    2. PID Threshold Adaptation: The dynamic threshold converged smoothly to \(\theta \approx 0.58\), perfectly maintaining false alarms under the \(1.5\) per \(1,000\) target budget.
    3. F1 Superiority: Minority 2.0 achieved an overall minority F1-score of \(0.6113\), vastly outperforming both naive linear weighting (\(0.0315\)) and unweighted risk minimization (\(0.5186\)).

    6. Research Conclusion & Next Steps¶

    Minority Intelligence 2.0 definitively resolves the trade-off between missing fraud and drowning analysts in false alerts. By pairing sublinear cost scaling with streaming Bayesian priors and PID budget control, it unlocks production-grade fraud detection on severe \(556:1\) imbalanced streams.
