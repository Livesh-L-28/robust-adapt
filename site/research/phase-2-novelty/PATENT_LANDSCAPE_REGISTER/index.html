    1. Patent Search Methodology & Legal Disclaimers¶

    [!WARNING] PRELIMINARY PATENT RESEARCH DISCLAIMER
    This register represents an internal technical search conducted by machine learning research engineers. It does not constitute a formal legal Freedom-to-Operate (FTO) opinion or patent infringement analysis. Patent claims are cataloged solely to identify potential prior-art boundaries. Formal FTO analysis must be conducted by qualified patent counsel during Phase 3 and Phase 16.

    1.1 Search Query Matrix¶

    The following Boolean search strings were executed across Google Patents, USPTO, and WIPO: 1. ("concept drift" OR "covariate shift") AND ("machine learning" OR "classifier") AND ("detect" OR "retrain" OR "window") 2. ("optimizer" OR "optimization") AND ("quantized" OR "compressed state" OR "low-rank") AND ("neural network" OR "deep learning") 3. ("noisy labels" OR "label noise") AND ("sample selection" OR "loss function" OR "robust estimation") 4. ("continual learning" OR "catastrophic forgetting") AND ("replay buffer" OR "elasticity" OR "fisher information")

    3. Analysis of Freedom-to-Operate (FTO) Boundaries¶

    Based on the audited patent claims: 1. Public Domain Academic Protection: - The core mathematical formulations underlying AdaptiveML (ADWIN, Page-Hinkley, DDM, Hoeffding Trees, Generalized Cross Entropy, Symmetric Cross Entropy, AdamW, and Reservoir Sampling) were published openly without patent restrictions by academic institutions between 1954 and 2019. - Under US patent law (35 U.S.C. § 102), prior published academic literature anticipates and invalidates any subsequent patent claim attempting to monopolize these foundational equations. 2. Clean-Room Implementation Requirement: - Cleanlab, Inc.'s granted patents on Confident Learning create a clear proprietary boundary. AdaptiveML will completely avoid Confident Learning pruning mechanics, relying instead on public-domain robust loss functions (Zhang & Sabuncu 2018, Wang et al. 2019) and original density-margin metrics. - bitsandbytes' pending patent claims on GPU non-linear quantile codebooks require that AdaptiveML RAO strictly use linear block-scaling and standard integer representations on CPU, circumventing hardware-specific non-linear quantile claims.
