    Primary Research Dossier 2: Robust Learning Algorithms, Noisy Labels & Imbalance¶

    Document Status: Complete Baseline Deliverable (Phase 4)
    Document Identifier: DOC-LIT-002
    Last Updated: 2026-11-01
    Research Pillar: Pillar 2 — RLA (Robust Learning Algorithms)

    1. Research Background & Problem Formulation¶

    Standard supervised machine learning relies on Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM). Given training set \(S = \{(x_i, \tilde{y}_i)\}_{i=1}^N\), where \(\tilde{y}_i\) denotes the observed (potentially corrupted) label: $\(\mathcal{R}_{\text{emp}}(f) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathcal{L}(f(x_i), \tilde{y}_i)\)$

    When labels are clean, ERM converges to the Bayes optimal classifier under cross-entropy loss. However, deep neural networks and flexible non-parametric estimators have sufficient capacity to memorize random labels (Zhang et al., ICLR 2017). When trained with standard Cross-Entropy on corrupted labels, models overfit to noise, degrading test-set generalization.

    Comprehensive Label Noise Taxonomy¶

    1. Symmetric (Uniform) Noise: A label flips to any other class with equal probability \(\eta / (K - 1)\): $\(P(\tilde{y} = j | y^* = i) = \begin{cases} 1 - \eta & \text{if } j = i \\ \frac{\eta}{K-1} & \text{if } j \ne i \end{cases}\)$
    2. Asymmetric (Class-Conditional) Noise: Flipping probabilities depend on the true latent class \(i\) (e.g. Cat \(\leftrightarrow\) Dog, Beaver \(\leftrightarrow\) Otter). Captured by a transition matrix \(T_{i, j} = P(\tilde{y} = j | y^* = i)\).
    3. Instance-Dependent (Feature-Dependent) Noise: The most realistic real-world noise model. The probability of an annotation error depends directly on feature difficulty \(x\): $\(P(\tilde{y} = j | y^* = i, x) = \eta_{i, j}(x)\)$ (e.g., borderline or low-resolution medical images are more frequently misannotated).

    4. Mathematical Mechanisms of Major Algorithms¶

    4.1 Generalized Cross Entropy (GCE)¶

    Zhang & Sabuncu (NeurIPS 2018) proposed utilizing the negative Box-Cox transformation as an interpolation between Cross-Entropy and Mean Absolute Error: $\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{GCE}}(f(x), y) = \frac{1 - f_y(x)^q}{q}, \quad q \in (0, 1]\)$ - Gradient Formulation: $\(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{GCE}}}{\partial \theta} = - f_y(x)^{q-1} \cdot \frac{\partial f_y(x)}{\partial \theta} = f_y(x)^q \cdot \left( -\frac{1}{f_y(x)} \frac{\partial f_y(x)}{\partial \theta} \right) = f_y(x)^q \cdot \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}\)$ The gradient of GCE is equivalent to standard Cross-Entropy scaled by \(f_y(x)^q\). For corrupt labels where the model prediction \(f_y(x)\) is low, the gradient contribution is automatically attenuated to near zero.

    4.2 Symmetric Cross Entropy (SCE)¶

    Wang et al. (ICCV 2019) proved that standard Cross-Entropy overfits because it is not symmetric. They paired CE with Reverse Cross Entropy (RCE): $\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{SCE}} = \alpha \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(p, y) + \beta \mathcal{L}_{\text{RCE}}(p, y) = -\alpha \sum_{k=1}^K y_k \ln p_k - \beta \sum_{k=1}^K p_k \ln y_k\)$ By parameterizing \(\ln y_k = A\) if \(y_k = 1\) and \(B\) if \(y_k = 0\), RCE acts as a symmetric regularizer with a bounded supremum: \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{RCE}} \le -A - (K-1)B\), preventing gradient explosion on mislabeled instances.

    4.3 Class-Balanced Loss (Effective Number of Samples)¶

    Cui et al. (CVPR 2019) resolved the distortion of naive inverse-frequency weighting by formalizing sample volume overlap in high-dimensional space: $\(E_n = \frac{1 - \beta^n}{1 - \beta}, \quad W_{n_y} = \frac{1}{E_{n_y}} = \frac{1 - \beta}{1 - \beta^{n_y}}\)$ where \(\beta = \frac{N-1}{N} \in [0, 1)\) represents the probability that a new sample overlaps with existing volume.

    6. The Core Research Breakdown: The Small-Loss Dilemma¶

    Every state-of-the-art sample selection and noise-filtering system in current literature relies on the Small-Loss Criterion: $\(\ell(x_i, y_i) < \tau \implies \text{Clean}; \quad \ell(x_i, y_i) \ge \tau \implies \text{Noisy / Prune}\)$

    Why This Fails Under Real-World Class Imbalance¶

    1. Minority-class instances (e.g. rare disease symptoms, rare financial fraud vectors) have very few supporting exemplars in the training set.
    2. Because representations for sparse classes are under-formed during early training, their training loss \(\ell(x_{\text{minority}}, y_{\text{minority}})\) remains persistently high.
    3. Small-loss algorithms (Cleanlab, Co-teaching, MentorNet) classify these legitimate rare samples as "noisy labels" and systematically prune or downweight them.
    4. Result: The classifier starves the minority class of data, destroying minority recall.

    7. Direct Relationship to AdaptiveML¶

    AdaptiveML Pillar 2 (RLA) addresses this breakdown by: 1. Providing clean-room, vectorized implementations of Generalized Cross Entropy (GCE) and Symmetric Cross Entropy (SCE) for both PyTorch and NumPy. 2. Formulating a Density-Aware Margin Reliability Metric that normalizes sample loss against local feature-space density: $\(\text{Score}(x_i, y_i) = \mathcal{M}(x_i, y_i) \cdot \log\left( 1 + \frac{1}{\rho_k(x_i)} \right)\)$ High-loss samples residing in sparse feature regions are identified as Legitimate Rare Samples and protected from deletion.
